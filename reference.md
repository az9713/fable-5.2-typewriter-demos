**Body as simple solids** (shape words are GUESS unless a source is given)

- An open iron frame, almost a cube. 4 corner posts, a base rectangle and a top plate with a round hole.
- In the round hole: the type basket. The type arms hang in a ring. "38 to 42 of these type arms", pivots in 2 levels, each arm with 2 characters [S7].
- On top: the carriage. It is a long frame that holds a rubber cylinder (platen), a wood feed roller and a metal paper shelf behind the platen [S6][S8].
- Front: the keyboard slopes down to the typist. Key levers are wood [S7]. The space bar is "the long wood bar in front of the keys" [S6].
- 2 ribbon spools, one at the left and one at the right of the basket. The ribbon winds from one spool to the other [S6]. Ribbon path: GUESS across the top of the basket, below the platen. Spool axis direction: GUESS front-to-back.
#### Material and finish

- Many parts were nickel plated, others japanned and ornamented; steel parts were blued (Scientific American 1888, in [S7]; the words are from a summary). Japanned = black baked lacquer.
- The decorations on the front "appear to be hand-painted" [S7]. Colour of decoration: GUESS gold lines and small flowers.
- Text on lower front edge of frame: "REMINGTON STANDARD TYPEWRITER NO. 2" [S1].
- Text on paper shelf: "KEEP THE MACHINE FREE FROM DUST. CLEAN ALL THE TOP RODS -- ESPECIALLY THE SHIFTING ROD -- WITH A GREASY CLOTH EVERY DAY" [S2][S5], and "TO SAVE TIME IS TO LENGTHEN LIFE" [S8].

#### Body as simple solids
- A low oak box (the base) with square corners. The top plate goes to the edges of the box [S23].
- A lid (oak) that the user removes to play [S31a]. 4 suitcase clips hold the lid: 2 at the front, 2 at the back [S23]. Lid shape: "dome lid" [S31a]. One source says "hinged lid" with a handle [S31b] (see "Contradictions").
- On the top plate: a horizontal tapered brass cylinder (the mandrel). Brass mandrel = early machines; nickel by 1898 [S25][S26].
- The feed screw is on the same axis as the mandrel [S26][S30].
- A carriage ("speaker arm") goes across above the mandrel. It slides on a back rod and rests on a straight edge at the front [S27]. It holds a round metal reproducer with a small tube on top [S30].
- A swing arm ("end gate") at the right end holds the right end of the mandrel shaft. It has a lock bolt [S27].
- A conical horn, 14 in, fits on the reproducer tube [S23][S27].
- A crank on the side of the box: "original slotted crank" [S24]; "black cast-iron handle with slots" [S23] (OCR text; it does not say which handle). Side: GUESS right.
- A leather belt goes from the motor in the box up to the mandrel pulley [S27][S28].
#### Materials and colours
- Case: oak [S23][S30]. Finish colour: GUESS mid golden-brown.
- Top plate: "finished in black and gold" [S23]; bedplate paint with pinstripe [S24].
- Lid decal (not on very early machines): banner with "EDISON HOME PHONOGRAPH" in red letters on gold [S23][S32]. The decal is on the lid until 1901 [S25].
- Horn: "14 in brass horn" [S23]; another machine has a "14 in black with brass bell horn" [S24].
- Brown wax cylinder: "tan to dark chocolate brown" [S30]. The surface is soft; it wore out after about 20 plays [S29].
- Governor: brass balls (early) [S25].
#### Controls and use (from the 1898 instruction book [S27])
1. Wind the crank. One winding plays 6 records [S23].
2. Lift the lift lever. Open the swing arm. Push the cylinder onto the mandrel, bevelled end first. Close and lock the swing arm.
3. Put the horn on the reproducer tube. Slide the speaker arm to the start of the record.
4. Push the start and stop switch to the left. Lower the lift lever.
5. To stop: lift the lift lever and move the switch back (direction: DERIVED, opposite of start).
- Speed adjusting screw: on the top, in plain sight on Model A [S28]. Start lever and speed control are separate controls above the top plate [S23].
- Speed: "about 125 revolutions a minute" for music [S27]; 120 rpm nominal [S28][S29]. At 120 rpm, 1 turn = 0.5 s (DERIVED).
- Playing time: about 2 minutes [S29][S31a].
#### What moves while it plays
- The mandrel and cylinder turn [S27]. The belt and the pulley turn [S27].
- The speaker arm and horn move slowly along the feed screw [S30]. Direction: DERIVED, left to right ([S27]: "slide to the left" to reach the start of the record). Travel in 2 minutes: GUESS 60 mm (assumes 100 threads per inch, not sourced).
- The governor balls spin [S25]. The gear work is "open and visible" on early machines [S31b]. If the governor is visible with the case closed: GUESS no.

## Contradictions between sources
1. **Remington No. 2 height.** 280 to 292 mm [S3][S4][S5] against 394 to 406 mm [S1][S2]. Possible cause (my reading): the Henry Ford values include a raised carriage or a different axis order.
2. **Remington No. 2 carriage-lever side.** Right side [S8] against left side (Scientific American 1888 text about the No. 3, in [S7]).
3. **Remington No. 2, how to lift the carriage.** By the curved lever [S8] against "by taking hold of the carriage-frame, not the carriage-lever" [S6].
4. **Remington No. 2, what moves at shift.** The platen moves on the carriage [S6][S7] against "moves the carriage" [S8].
5. **Edison Home lid.** Removed, held by 4 clips [S23][S31a] against "hinged lid" [S31b].
6. **Edison Home speed.** "About 125" rpm [S27] against 120 rpm [S28][S29].
7. **Selectric weight.** 31 lb [S16] against 17 kg [S18].
8. **Selectric case material.** Die-cast aluminium [S17] against steel [S18].
9. **Selectric height.** 6.75 in (171 mm) [S17] against 190 mm [S18].
10. **AR XA platter weight.** 3.9 lb [S39] against 3.3 lb [S44]. One source says "five pounds total" for the unit [S44b] against 13.5 lb [S39].
11. **Fluorescent light colour.** 5000 K [S48] against 4100 K [S66] (SEARCH-ONLY).
12. **Remington Portable depth.** 280 mm [S14] against 330 mm [S12] (the second value can include the baseboard).
